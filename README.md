# Classroom Games: Instructions

This site has two games for 3rd grade. Start on the home page and choose a game.

---

## Rocket Pizza Math

**Skills:** multiplication facts and fractions

**Goal:** Answer questions correctly to fill the rocket's fuel tank. Every 5 correct answers launches the rocket.

### How to play

1. Choose a game:
   - **Rocket Math:** multiplication questions.
   - **Pizza Fractions:** fraction questions about a pizza.
2. For Rocket Math, pick the times tables to practice (0s to 12s). Click **Clear all** or **Select all** to change them quickly. Pizza Fractions does not use this setting.
   For Pizza Fractions, pick the pizza slice counts to practice (2 to 12; the bottom number of the fraction), using the same **Clear all** / **Select all** button. Then choose the question type: **Name it** (name the shaded fraction, from 0 slices shaded up to all of them), **Simplify it** (write a fraction in simplest form), or **Mix** of both. Simplify questions only use slice counts that can be simplified (4, 6, 8, 9, 10, 12). If none of those are chosen, you get Name it questions instead. Rocket Math does not use these settings.
3. Click **Start game**.
4. Answer by clicking an answer, or by pressing **1**, **2**, **3**, or **4** on the keyboard.
5. Click **Next question**, or press **Enter**, to keep going.
6. Each correct answer adds one segment of fuel. When all five segments are full, click **Launch rocket**, then **Keep flying**.
7. Click **End game** when you are finished to see your summary.

### Scoring

- Each correct answer earns **10 points**.
- **Timer:** every question has 10 seconds. The seconds left show above the question next to a shrinking bar, and turn red in the last 3 seconds. **If time runs out, the question counts as wrong** and the right answer is shown. Both games use the timer.
- **Speed bonus:** answering right away earns up to **10 extra points**, and the bonus shrinks to 0 as the timer runs down.
- Once you have **3 correct answers in a row**, each correct answer earns an extra **5 points**.
- A wrong answer, or running out of time, ends your streak. The fuel you have already earned stays in the tank.
- Your best score is saved on the device you are using.

## Storm Chaser Robot

**Skills:** counting moves, planning the shortest route, and comparing totals

**Goal:** Deliver the package from the **depot** (bottom left) to the **house** (top right) using as few moves as possible. There are six cities, and each one is harder than the last.

### How to play

1. Type a name for your robot (up to 12 letters). The default name is **Bolt**.
2. Click **Start first delivery**.
3. Move one square per press with the **arrow keys** or **W, A, S, D**. You can also click the arrow buttons.
4. Trees and rocks block the way. The robot cannot move into them or off the edge of the map.
5. Watch the **Moves** counter. Plan your route before you move.
6. When the robot reaches the house, click **Next delivery**.
7. Click **Restart route** to start the current delivery over.
8. After a delivery, click **Show best route** to see the shortest route for that city.

### Weather

| What you see | What it means | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Blue stripes (rain) | The robot is slowed by rain. | Each step onto rain costs **2 moves**. |
| Light blue arrow (wind) | Wind blows the robot one square in the arrow's direction. | The robot moves to the next square in that direction. If a tree or rock is in the way, the robot stays on the wind square. |
| Pushing against the arrow | The robot pushes through the gust. | That step costs **2 moves**. The robot is not blown. |

### Feedback on the board

- A green **+1** means a normal step.
- A red **+2 rain** or **+2 push** shows what the weather cost you.
- **Gust!** means the robot ran into a wind square.

### Stars

- **3 stars:** your route used the fewest possible moves.
- **2 stars:** you were within 2 moves of the fewest possible.
- **1 star:** you took more than 2 extra moves.

There are 18 stars in total (3 for each of the six cities). Your best total is saved on the device you are using.

---

## Notes for teachers

- Scores are saved in each browser, so they stay on the device where the game was played.
- Ask students to predict the shortest route before they move. Then compare their move count with the **Best possible** number.
